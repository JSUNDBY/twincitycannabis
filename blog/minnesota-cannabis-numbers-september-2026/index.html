Minnesota cannabis by the numbers: what our September data shows

An eighth still costs fifty dollars. But the market quietly split into two pricing worlds this month, and vape carts finally showed up. Here is what a month of tracking says.

Once a month we stop and write down what our own data says about Minnesota's legal market. No spin, no predictions, just what is on the shelves and what it costs. Everything below is from our tracking as of mid September 2026. Where we could not verify a number we left it out, and where a number moved for a boring reason we say so instead of dressing it up.

The product count doubled, mostly not for the reason you would guess

In August we tracked about 2,780 products. Today it is 5,702. That is not the market doubling in five weeks. Most of it is us. We spent the month getting menus flowing from shops we already had listed but could not read, and the dispensary count only went from 144 to 156 in the same stretch.

So treat that jump as better eyesight, not a boom. The useful part is that the picture is far less patchy now, especially outside the metro.

138 shops we can confirm are actually open

We hold 156 dispensary records across 88 Minnesota cities. Of those, 138 clear our bar for confirmed operating, which is the number the site shows you. The metro and greater Minnesota split almost evenly at 76 and 80, and that split has held all year. It still surprises people who assume this is a Twin Cities market with a few outstate stragglers attached.

If you have not looked lately, the directory is worth another pass, and open now will tell you who is taking customers this minute.

An eighth still costs fifty dollars

Across every eighth of flower on the tracker the median is $49.97. In August it was right around $50. Five weeks and several hundred new listings later, the middle of the market has not moved a dollar.

The tenth percentile sits at $40 and the ninetieth at $60. So the honest range for a normal eighth is forty to sixty, and fifty is the number to carry in your head. If you are paying well past sixty you are either buying something genuinely special or you are paying for the storefront. The cheapest flower pages track the bottom of that range, and the price medians page has the rest.

The market split into two pricing worlds

This is the most interesting thing in the September data, and it would be easy to misread.

Of the 300 products carried at three or more shops, 201 are priced identically at every shop that stocks them. In August almost nothing was. That looks like prices converging across Minnesota. It is not. It is chains arriving.

Green Goods now has eight Minnesota locations, and 91 of the 102 products it carries at three or more of its own stores are the same price at all of them. Same jar, same price in Minneapolis, Rochester, Moorhead and Duluth. That is one price list run statewide.

Take the chains back out and the independents look exactly like they always have. Purple Ice Water sits on eleven menus from $42 to $58. A Brownie Scout half gram is on thirteen and runs $30 to $65.

So the shopping rule now depends on which kind of shop you are standing in. At a chain, comparing locations is a waste of your time, because the price is the price. At independents, comparing is worth more than it has ever been. That is what the price spread index is for.

Vape carts finally showed up

In August we counted 49 cartridges statewide and said the category should fill in over the next year. It took five weeks. There are 242 now, and it is the fastest growing shelf on the site.

One warning before you go shopping on it. Half gram and full gram carts sit side by side on menus, often with the size buried or missing entirely. The median half gram is $58.25. The median full gram is $64.00. That is $5.75 more for twice the oil, which makes the half gram close to double the price per gram. And 41 cartridge listings on our tracker show no size at all.

Do the per gram math every single time. You can browse cartridges here or start with the cheapest cartridges in Minneapolis.

What we would actually do with this

Keep fifty dollars in your head for an eighth. Check the size on a cart before you look at the price. If you shop a chain, stop comparing its locations, and if you shop independents, compare every time, because a twenty dollar swing on the same jar is still sitting right there. And if you live outside the metro, look again, because what we can see out there got a lot better this month.
