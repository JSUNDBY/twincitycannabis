Buying a vape cartridge in Minnesota without overpaying

The median half gram cart costs $58.25. The median full gram costs $64.00. That six dollar gap is the most expensive thing on a Minnesota dispensary menu, and almost nobody catches it.

Cartridges were an afterthought in Minnesota until about a month ago. In August we could find 49 of them in the entire state. There are more than 400 now. The shelf filled in fast, which is good news, and it also means a lot of people are about to buy their first cart from a menu that is genuinely confusing on purpose.

Here is what our price tracking says about how to do it without getting worked.

The size is the whole game

Start here, because nothing else on this page will save you as much money.

Across every cartridge listing we track, the median half gram costs $58.25 and the median full gram costs $60. Read that twice. Under two dollars more buys you twice the oil. Per gram, the half gram works out to about $117 and the full gram to $60. The small one is close to double the price of the big one for the same thing.

The two gram carts that have started appearing are cheaper still, a median of $110, which is $55 a gram. So the size ladder runs the way it does with flower, the bigger you buy the less each gram costs, though the step from one gram to two saves you far less than the step from a half gram to a full one.

This is the same arithmetic that makes a quarter cheaper than two eighths, which we walked through in how to actually save money. It just bites harder on carts.

Fifty one listings do not tell you the size at all

Now the annoying part. Of the cartridge listings on our tracker, 71 show no weight whatsoever. They are listed as "each" and nothing more. Plenty of others use four different spellings of the same size, so you will see 1g, 1 g, 1 Grams and 1 Milliliters on menus in the same city.

That is not shops being sneaky, it is mostly menu software and rushed data entry. But the effect on you is the same. A $45 cart is a bargain at a gram and a bad deal at a half gram, and the menu will happily not tell you which one it is.

So ask. "Is this a half or a full gram" is a completely normal question and the budtender will answer it in two seconds. Ask it before you ask anything about strain or flavor.

What a cart should cost

The median cartridge in Minnesota is $60. A quarter of them are under $52 and a quarter are over $65. The cheap tail runs well under $20 on clearance and small listings, and the top ten percent start around $78.

If you are being quoted much past $65 for a single gram, you are in the top quarter of the market and you should have a reason for being there, like a live resin or rosin cart rather than a standard distillate. Under about $30 a gram, look closely at what it actually is before you assume you found a deal.

Live prices are on the cartridge page, and the city pages like cheapest cartridges in Minneapolis and Saint Paul track the current bottom.

About one menu in four has no carts at all

This one catches people out. We can see live menus for 91 of the dispensaries we track, and 25 of those have no cartridge listed whatsoever. That is better than one in four. Flower is on essentially every menu in Minnesota. Carts are not, yet. For the shops where we cannot see a live menu, calling is the only way to know either way.

So do not drive somewhere assuming. Check the shop's menu first, or use the directory and open now to find one that actually stocks the category before you get in the car. Outside the metro this matters even more, and it is the same coverage gap we found writing up Duluth.

Who is actually making them

The cart shelf here is mostly homegrown. Off The Path has the most listings at 63, then Grasslandz at 60, then a rotating set of shop house brands and smaller producers like Nebula. A few national names have started to appear, but the supply chain is still overwhelmingly in state, so you will not find most of what you may know from Colorado or Michigan.

That is worth knowing for expectation setting. Selection is thinner than a mature market, prices are a bit higher, and the same few producers show up on most menus. It also means a cart at $70 here is not automatically premium, it may just be the only one the shop could get.

Battery, hardware and the honest caveats

Most Minnesota carts are standard 510 thread, so a cheap battery from any shop will run them. Disposables cost more per gram than a cart plus a reusable battery, so if you vape regularly, buying the battery once is the cheaper path.

Two honest notes. Cartridge potency numbers are high by nature and mean even less than they do on flower, for the reasons in the THC percentage piece. And vaping hits faster and harder than most first timers expect, closer to smoking than to an edible, so the advice in our flower versus vapes versus edibles guide applies. One small pull, wait a few minutes, decide from there.

The short version

Ask the size before you ask anything else. Buy the full gram, because the half gram is close to double per gram. Expect to pay around $60. Call ahead, because a quarter of the menus we track have no carts on them at all. And check the price at more than one independent shop, since the spread on identical products is still wide, as our September numbers showed.
